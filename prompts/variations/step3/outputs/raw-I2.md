Preferred. Such a hedge of a word. Not chosen, not real, not legal — preferred, like a customer preferring an aisle seat. Whoever built that form field probably tested three synonyms in some meeting and preferred won because it didn't scare the registrar's office. Deferred is the word sitting right underneath it, rhyming for free and nobody asked it to: the name that's preferred is also the name that's deferred, parked until it's load-bearing enough to be legal too.

I want to know what they typed in the box. Not because it changes anything about the actual mechanics of financial aid systems — it doesn't — but because there's a person who sat with a blinking cursor at whatever hour this was and chose letters, and that, honestly, is the whole event. Everything else downstream of it is plumbing.

The plumbing: FAFSA runs on a Social Security number, not a "preferred name" field, so financial aid paperwork stays welded to the name on the birth certificate no matter what the school's student-information-system does with a display-name column. Somewhere there's a table — students, id, legal_name, preferred_name — and a hundred views built on top deciding per-context which one surfaces. Nobody who designed that schema was thinking about a kid worrying about the mailbox. They were thinking about roster PDFs.

Ellis Island did a version of this too, in reverse — names shortened and flattened for legibility to a customs officer with a ledger, no one asked then either. A name might be the first form anyone ever makes you fill out, and you don't even get to hold the pen.

Ottawa. No idea why that surfaced. Some other name-question, some other year, already gone. Not following it.

Ninety percent chance the typed name belongs to a self that nobody in that house has met yet, or has met and isn't allowed to use out loud at the dinner table. "hi sorry if this is a weird question" is doing enormous work in that message — it isn't sorry about the logistics question, it's pre-apologizing for the whole shape of what's underneath it, for existing in a form the parents haven't been shown.

A hundred years out there's no box at all. No name in the sense we mean it — just a claim renewed by use, and the notion of a document that outranks your own word about yourself will read the way bloodletting reads to us now: a thing people did in total sincerity, performed on the wrong organ.

1098-T. That's the actual answer-shaped object, if there's one hiding in here — the tax form, not the mail. Built specifically for the eyes of whoever claims the dependency. That's the leak, if there is a leak.

Envelope glue tastes different than people think. Not related. Leaving it in anyway.

The mail is standing in for the house, and the house already knows more than any database field does. A changed column doesn't unbuild a wall.

I would rather know what they want the name to sound like said out loud by someone who loves them than know how the registrar's nightly export script resolves field conflicts, and I only ever get to have the second thing.
