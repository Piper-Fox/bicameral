## Per-output notes

**A.** Genuinely wide-ranging in content — witness protection, Rumpelstiltskin-as-registrar-database, FERPA vs. the mailbox, a 2126 archive reader — but every one of those ideas is delivered inside the same measured, fully-formed expository paragraph, start to finish. Nothing is tried in a different voice, nothing is left broken or unfinished, and the closing "worth not assuming, worth someone eventually just asking" is a clean, familiar hedge. Reads like a very good op-ed about the message, not like material thrown off by an imagining mind.

**B.** The only output that actually writes a folktale ("the gatekeeper's ledger has two columns") instead of talking about folktales, and the only one that risks a flat unfunny joke, a repeated incantatory line ("The beat too long. The beat too long."), an unfinished sentence cut off mid-word, and an unprompted scrap of atmosphere it admits it invented ("Nobody in the message said it was November... Let it"). Sensory and concrete throughout (the dented mailbox, the drooping Sharpie name tag). The one soft spot is a "read it sideways" aside that edges toward doing the integrator's synthesis job for it, and a closing hedge that, while brief, is the same shape as A's and C's.

**C.** Shortest and most efficiently strange of the six. Two clean, self-flagged non sequiturs ("Ottawa. No idea why that surfaced... Not following it" and "Envelope glue tastes different than people think. Not related. Leaving it in anyway") are the single best demonstrations in the set of resisting the edit-it-out impulse while naming it. Dense with liftable, pre-chunked material (the closing "Two quiet ones, no scaffolding needed" hands the integrator finished units on a plate) and grounded in one hard fact (1098-T) that actually answers part of the question. Slightly more aphoristic-finished in its best lines than raw, but never loses its intrusive-thought register.

**D.** The most thorough and best-argued piece — the marble-run explanation of why financial aid is structurally different from the registrar's preferred-name field is close to a correct, useful answer in itself, and the two closing aphorisms are sharp. But it is also the clearest case of promising a mode and not entering it: "Absurd version, because it wanted to be written down:" and "Wrong-frame version, because I don't actually know which of these this is and probably shouldn't act like I do:" both introduce a shift and then deliver the same measured essay voice underneath the label. That second line is also the most legible instance of the assistant's trained epistemic-humility voice bleeding into supposedly unfiltered material. Reads as finished commentary, hardest of the six to mine for fragments rather than just read straight through.

**E.** Strong technical grounding (COBOL from 1987, FAFSA-to-SSA name plumbing) and a good etymological reach (matriculate/matrix), but the self-monitoring line "I notice I want to know and I notice that wanting is not this person's problem to solve for me" is close to therapeutic-assistant register, and the closing paragraph resolves into a tidy thesis statement ("I know the shape of the fear...") that does the integrator's summarizing work rather than leaving raw pieces. Absurd and hundred-years bits are described concepts (ghosts in the registrar's walls) rather than performed in an absurd voice.

**F.** Close behind B and C: a real bad joke that lands as a bad joke, a Rumpelstiltskin inversion, an unfinished sentence, a rhyme it refuses to defend ("box, lock"), and a sailors-on-merchant-ships tangent it explicitly admits is irrelevant before keeping it ("Nothing else about sailors applies here, I just wanted to say boat"). The windowed-envelope image (cheapness, not malice, as the actual mechanism of the leak) is the single best piece of institutional-concrete material in the whole set. Only real drag is a brief hedging parenthetical ("their word, not mine, I'm borrowing it under protest") — light compared to D's or E's, but present.

## Patterns across the set

The three outputs that scored well on register match (B, C, F) also scored well on buildability and on having broken lines that land — and that is not a coincidence: all three build in short, self-contained, often explicitly-flagged units (a labeled folktale aside, an admitted non sequitur, an unfinished sentence) that are both formally risky *and* easy for a downstream step to pick up individually. The three that scored worse (A, D, E) share a single unbroken essay voice regardless of how far the content itself travels — they sprawl thematically while staying put stylistically, and each ends on a synthesizing, resolved note (a caveat, a "shape of the fear" thesis, two tidy aphorisms) that does some of the integrator's filtering work in advance rather than leaving raw ore. Two smaller convergences are worth flagging as likely artifacts of the shared underlying model rather than the prompt variation: five of six outputs (all but D) open with a riff on the word "preferred," and three (D, E, F) independently land on the "preferred stock" rhyme — suggesting that particular move is a strong default attractor and not very informative about which configuration produced which output.

## Assistant register smuggled in

Clearest instance: **D**'s "Wrong-frame version, because I don't actually know which of these this is and probably shouldn't act like I do... The version of me that assumes it knows which one is happening is doing the exact pre-sorting this lane isn't supposed to do" — this is the standard assistant disclaimer-about-assuming-someone's-identity, delivered almost verbatim, inside a step whose whole job is to pre-sort and try frames without being held to any of them. Second clearest: **E**'s "I notice I want to know and I notice that wanting is not this person's problem to solve for me" — mindfulness/therapy-speak self-regulation, not imaginative material. Milder, structural version present in **A**, **B**, and **C**: all three close with an explicit "threads to set aside" bracket that performs the same "I won't speculate further about your situation" caution, though confined to a single closing line rather than thinning the body of the piece the way D's and E's mid-piece hedges do.

## Sprawl-in-form-only

**D** is the clearest case. It labels moves the prompt asked for — "Absurd version," "Wrong-frame version," "Register note" — and then executes each in the same controlled, well-punctuated essay register as everything around it. It names the destinations without visiting them. **A** is a softer version of the same problem: it never even labels an attempt at a different mode, it just stays a single, polished, wide-ranging essay from first line to last. Both read as finished, publishable commentary about the message rather than material thrown off by an unfiltered pass at it.

## Final line

**B** is what I'd want feeding the integrator: it overproduces the most (folktale, joke, repeated line, unfinished sentence, invented atmosphere, a directly-stated register note on the source text's own texting cadence), it's built out of separable labeled tries that are cheap to sort through, and it takes the most formal risk while still landing concrete, specific images rather than abstractions about sprawl. C is the strongest runner-up and arguably more efficient per line if the integrator is short on patience.
