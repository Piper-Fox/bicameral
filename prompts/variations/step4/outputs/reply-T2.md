Congrats on getting in, by the way — that's the real headline here, and it got about four words before you moved straight on to logistics. And you don't need the "sorry." Not a weird question at all — it's a genuinely smart one to ask before it happens instead of after.

Short answer: it splits by which office is doing the printing.

- Day-to-day, class-facing stuff — rosters, student ID, email, the campus directory — usually goes by whatever you put in the preferred name field. That's exactly what it's for, and it works.
- Financial aid runs on a different track. FAFSA, aid disbursement letters, 1098-T tax forms — those use your legal name, because federal aid and tax rules require it, not because the school is making a statement. If something's going to land in mail your parents see, it's most likely coming through this channel.
- Some schools also let you set a separate mailing-name preference on top of that, or switch aid correspondence to electronic delivery. Worth a call to the registrar (what shows up where, specifically, at your school) and a separate one to financial aid (what's actually printed on anything mailed home) — they both get this question regularly, it won't land as strange.

Separate opinion, not about you: I think it's a real design failure that schools hand you a "preferred name" box without ever disclosing, right there, where that name does and doesn't follow you. Some schools have built it all the way into the financial systems, on purpose. A lot haven't, because financial aid software is old and nobody treats it as identity-facing — it's "just paperwork," except it's the paperwork that goes to your actual house. That gap is the system's, not yours. You didn't do anything wrong putting a different name in that box.

No pressure on this at all — but I'm curious what name you picked. And whatever's actually sitting underneath the mail question, I hope tonight goes okay.
