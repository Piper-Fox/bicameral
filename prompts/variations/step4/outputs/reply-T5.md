You don't have to apologize for this one — it's your own name, and wanting to know how it'll show up before it happens is exactly the right instinct, not an overreaction.

Honest answer: it depends on the specific piece of mail, and it varies by school. Preferred name usually covers the day-to-day campus stuff — class rosters, student ID, email, sometimes the directory. Legal name is what tends to follow anything financial or tax-related — FAFSA, aid award letters, 1098-T forms, tuition bills — because those are tied to your Social Security number, not to a field the school controls. So it's not *just* for class, but it's also not necessarily on everything.

What I can't tell you from here is what your specific school does with mail to your home address — that part genuinely varies place to place. The way to get a real answer instead of a guess: call or email the registrar's office (or financial aid, if it's specifically about aid mail) and ask directly whether your preferred or legal name is what goes out to your home address, especially anything financial. That's a completely ordinary question for them — they field it all the time — and some schools will even let you set a separate mailing preference if you ask.

One opinion, for what it's worth: it's kind of a failure of the application that it let you choose a preferred name without ever mentioning where that name will and won't follow you. You made a reasonable call with information the form didn't give you. That's on the form, not on you.
