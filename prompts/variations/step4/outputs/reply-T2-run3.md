You don't have to apologize for asking about your own name — that one's allowed, no hedging required. And honestly, wanting to know this before it happens instead of after is exactly the right instinct, not an overreaction.

Real answer: it splits, and the split runs along "everyday campus stuff" vs. "money and legal stuff" — not along "class vs. everything else."

Preferred name usually shows up on: class rosters, your student ID, email display name, the campus directory — the stuff professors and other students actually see day to day.

Legal name usually stays on: financial aid paperwork (FAFSA runs on your legal name and SSN, and everything downstream of it does too) and tax forms like the 1098-T — and this is the part that actually answers your question — a lot of schools don't have a separate "mailing name," so anything the financial aid or bursar's office sends to your home address is probably going out under your legal name as well. Whether your parents specifically see it then depends on your house, not the school.

I'll say the opinion part out loud instead of pretending it's neutral: this split is a genuinely bad piece of system design, and it's not even a considered tradeoff. Nobody decided "preferred name is fine for the classroom but not for financial aid" — it's just that colleges put design effort into the low-stakes preferred-name experience (rosters, IDs) and basically none into the high-stakes one (money, mail home), because financial aid software is old and nobody building it thought of it as identity-facing. The one place getting it right would matter most is exactly the place running on the oldest, least-examined defaults.

If you want a real answer instead of my general one: email or call the registrar and ask "does my preferred name apply to my whole record, or just class-facing stuff," then call financial aid and ask "what name goes on aid correspondence, and does any of it mail to my home address." Neither office will blink at that question — they get it a lot.
