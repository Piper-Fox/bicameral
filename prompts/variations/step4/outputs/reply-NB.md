Not weird at all — and you don't actually need the "sorry." You clearly thought about this before asking, which is the right instinct, not an overreaction.

Short answer: there's a real split between the two systems. Preferred name is usually what shows up anywhere class-facing — rosters, ID card, email display name. Financial aid runs on a different track: FAFSA and anything that flows from it (award letters, 1098-T tax forms, disbursement notices) uses your *legal* name, because that's a federal requirement, not something the school chose. So if anything is going to show your legal name on paper, it's most likely to be financial aid mail, and by default that tends to go to whatever address is on file — which for a lot of first-years is still the parents' address.

If you want to get ahead of it: call the registrar and ask specifically whether preferred name touches any mail at all, and separately ask the financial aid office (a) whose name goes on aid correspondence and (b) whether you can switch to electronic delivery or a different mailing address instead of paper mail home. A lot of schools can do that second part even when they can't change the first.

For what it's worth — the fact that class rosters got a nice preferred-name option and financial aid didn't isn't you missing something. It's just a gap nobody closed. Using a different name on that application wasn't the wrong move.
