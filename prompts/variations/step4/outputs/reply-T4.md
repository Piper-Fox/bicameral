You really don't need to apologize for this one — it's not weird, and it's honestly a harder question to get a straight answer to than it should be.

Short version: it depends on your specific school, because colleges aren't consistent about this. But here's how it usually breaks down:

- Preferred name almost always shows up in the everyday stuff — class rosters, your student ID, email and directory listings, professors calling on you. That part's usually fine.
- Financial aid is the one to actually check. FAFSA and federal aid paperwork (like the 1098-T at tax time) run on your legal name, because that's tied to your Social Security number — and a lot of schools just mail financial aid correspondence to whatever's in that legal-name field, with no separate option. Some schools do let you set a different name or address for correspondence. Whether yours is one of them, I genuinely can't tell you.
- The fastest way to find out for sure, without making it a whole thing: email or call the registrar and ask "does financial aid mail go out under my legal name or my preferred name, and can I set something different for correspondence." That's a completely ordinary question for that office — nobody there will think twice about it.

For what it's worth, the reason this isn't a clean yes/no isn't anything you should've already known — it's a real gap in how a lot of these systems got built. Class rosters got the preferred-name treatment because nobody thought of them as high-stakes. Financial aid systems are older, and nobody ever went back and asked whether they should work the same way. So if it turns out your school does default to your legal name, that's not a decision anyone made about you on purpose — it's just an old default nobody's fixed.
