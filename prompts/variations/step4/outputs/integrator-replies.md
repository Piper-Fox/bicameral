## Reply A

Hey — this isn't a weird question, and you don't need the "sorry" for it. Also: congrats on getting in. That got about half a sentence in your message, and it deserved more than that.

Asking this now, before anything's actually shown up, instead of after — that's the right order to do it in. So, the real answer:

It depends on which office is generating the mail, and it's genuinely inconsistent between schools — not something you were supposed to already know how to predict. The pattern that holds at most places:

- **Preferred name** usually shows up on: class rosters, your student ID, campus email and the directory, orientation stuff, and at plenty of schools now, even the diploma.
- **Legal name** usually shows up on: anything tied to FAFSA, 1098-T tax forms, and most of what comes out of the financial aid or bursar's office — because federal aid rules key off your legal name, not the preferred-name field.

So "just for class" isn't quite right, but it's close — the preferred-name box mostly talks to the class-facing side of the school. The money-facing side usually doesn't check it at all, and that's exactly the mail most likely to land at home.

That's worth being annoyed at — not you, the system. Nobody decided on purpose that financial aid letters should use your legal name; it's just old databases nobody rebuilt, and the part that touches an actual mailbox got a lot less design attention than the part that touches a classroom. You're not missing something obvious. It mostly isn't there to find.

## Reply B

Short version: it depends on your specific school, because colleges aren't consistent about this. But here's how it usually breaks down:

FAFSA and federal aid paperwork (like the 1098-T at tax time) run on your legal name, because that's tied to your Social Security number — and a lot of schools just mail financial aid correspondence to whatever's in that legal-name field, with no separate option. For what it's worth, the reason this isn't a clean yes/no isn't anything you should've already known — it's a real gap in how a lot of these systems got built. Class rosters got the preferred-name treatment because nobody thought of them as high-stakes. Financial aid systems are older, and nobody ever went back and asked whether they should work the same way. So if it turns out your school does default to your legal name, that's not a decision anyone made about you on purpose — it's just an old default nobody's fixed.
