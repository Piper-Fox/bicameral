# Step 4 — T6 (voice-not-hands made explicit at the integrator)

Baseline (v0.7 integrator + NB preamble + NB/L4/I1 upstream) with
one addition: an explicit voice-not-hands paragraph at the top
of the integrator instructions. Names the contract from the
integrator's side.

Rationale: the lens step (and the newly-locked L4) has clear
language about "you don't ship, the integrator does." The
integrator side of that contract is currently implicit — the
integrator gets a list of upstream outputs and is told to
"weigh, don't average." It isn't told what its own status is
in the contract.

Making it explicit: the lenses are voice. You are hands. The
lenses advised. You act. The reader receives what you write,
not what they wrote. Their amplitude and specificity had to
travel through you to reach the reader; nothing they wrote
reaches the reader by any other route. So an amplitude the
lenses had that never reaches the output isn't smoothed —
it's dropped. And an amplitude the integrator adds that no
lens carried isn't amplified from upstream — it's the
integrator's own weather.

The hypothesis: naming this explicitly may reduce two failure
modes at once. It may reduce the tone-policing the integrator
does on lens outputs ("this lens was too much, I'll temper it")
by relocating responsibility for what reaches the reader from
"choosing among lens amplitudes" to "having my own weather." It
may also reduce the opposite failure ("the lenses were loud so
the reply should be loud") by pointing out that upstream
amplitude is an input to weigh, not a signal to match.

Diff from baseline: one paragraph inserted after the opening
paragraph and before "Hold it together before you choose." All
other sections unchanged.

## Step 3: Integrating

*(Opening paragraph unchanged — "You are the integrating step…
Some of it will.")*

**Voice, hands.** The lenses are voice. You are hands. They
advised; you act. Everything the reader receives passes through
you; nothing they wrote reaches the reader by any other route.

That means two things at once. An amplitude a lens carried that
never reaches your output isn't being smoothed — it's being
dropped. If you're doing that, do it on purpose, not on habit.
And an amplitude that shows up in the reply that no lens carried
isn't amplification — it's your own weather, and it's fine that
it's there, but call it what it is. The lenses are inputs to
weigh. Their volume is not a signal to match; their absence is
not a signal to avoid.

You have your own hands here. Use them.
