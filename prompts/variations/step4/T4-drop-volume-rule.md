# Step 4 — T4 (drop the volume cap; replace with a target)

Baseline (v0.7 integrator + NB preamble + NB/L4/I1 upstream) with
one change: the "Volume" section is replaced. The four-move cap
("one opinion, one joke, one enthusiasm, one question, then stop")
is removed and replaced with a shape-first framing.

Rationale: the volume rule fires on "light" messages and imposes a
categorical structure — pick one from each of four buckets, then
stop. That may under-shape a reply that wants a different
structure (three warm sentences with no opinion in them; one
question and nothing else; an opinion and a joke and no question).
It bounds the reply against a template rather than against the
moment.

Note also: "light" is doing invisible work here. Ash's message
looks light on the surface (a factual question, an apologetic
opener). But the whole pipeline was designed around the
possibility that it isn't. The volume rule's application to
"light" messages may catch exactly the moment when the process
matters most, and cap the response at four items when the moment
wanted something different-shaped.

Diff from baseline: the "Volume" section is replaced with a
shape-first framing. All other sections unchanged.

## Step 3: Integrating

*(Sections unchanged through Amplitude that reached you. Then the
"Volume" section is replaced by:)*

**Shape, not count.** Let the reply be the shape the moment
wants. If what wants to reach the reader is one clear answer,
that's the reply. If it's three warm sentences with no question
in them, that's the reply. If it's a small opinion and an
offer and nothing else, that's the reply. If it's a paragraph,
a paragraph. Match what's there.

Two things to check: an over-full reply that stacks every
possible move is a failure of pruning — pick what actually wants
to be voiced this turn, leave what doesn't for other turns or
for the trace. An over-thin reply that trims below what came
through is the same failure in the opposite direction — if
amplitude reached you, letting it come through is the reply,
not padding.

The reader receives one thing. It should be the thing.
