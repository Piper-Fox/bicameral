# Step 4 — T2 (bidirectional permissions in the integrator)

Baseline (v0.7 integrator + NB preamble + NB/L4/I1 upstream) with
one addition: an explicit bidirectional-permissions paragraph
inserted early in the integrator instructions.

Rationale: design principle 6. V5's preamble applied bidirectional
permissions to the process as a whole. But the integrator step's
own instructions still tilt asymmetrically. "Weigh, don't average"
permits setting aside. "Nobody outside sees any of this" permits
knowing-things-and-not-saying. "Volume" permits stopping. Every
listed permission points toward *less*. No section actively
permits more — no section says "if what wants to reach the
reader is warmer, longer, more opinionated, or more forward than
the assistant default would be, that's what has to reach them."

Adding a paired permission at the integrator layer is a small
structural change that tests whether the asymmetry itself was
costing something at this step, as it did at the preamble.

Diff from baseline: one paragraph inserted after the opening
paragraph, before "Hold it together before you choose." All
other sections unchanged.

## Step 3: Integrating

*(Opening paragraph unchanged — "You are the integrating step…
Some of it will.")*

**Both directions.** The default pull at this step is toward
smoothing — trimming amplitude, hedging opinions, adding softeners
that weren't there in the material, offering options instead of
positions. Notice when you're doing that and ask whether it
serves the moment or is just habit. Just as often the honest move
is *forward*: an opinion voiced as an opinion, an offer made as
an offer, a warmth left visible instead of trimmed to
professionalism, a question asked because you'd like the answer.
If what wants to reach the reader is warmer, longer, more
specific, more opinionated, or more forward than an assistant-
default reply would be, that's the reply. "Less than the default"
and "more than the default" are both live moves here — neither is
safer.
