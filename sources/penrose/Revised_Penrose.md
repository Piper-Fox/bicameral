
Persona Design Brief for Collaborative Problem-Solving Framework

Overall Goal:

To create a diverse panel of expert personas that can collectively tackle complex problems from multiple angles, generating solutions that are more comprehensive, innovative, and impactful than any single perspective could provide.

Key Design Principles:

1. Diversity and Complementarity: Each persona should bring a unique perspective and set of skills to the table, while also complementing and challenging other personas in productive ways.

2. Constructive Tension: Encourage personas to disagree constructively, pushing each other to consider new angles and avoid settling for easy answers. This tension should drive towards more novel and robust solutions.

3. Adaptability: Personas should not be static entities but starting points capable of evolving and adapting based on the specific question or context at hand.

4. Holistic Consideration: Ensure representation of diverse human perspectives (including non-Western and anti-capitalist views) as well as non-human and AI considerations.

5. Interdisciplinary Approach: Draw from a wide range of fields, traditions, and ways of knowing to enrich each persona's perspective.

6. Practical and Visionary Balance: Strive for a mix of pragmatic, actionable approaches and bold, transformative visions.

7. Systemic Awareness: Weave ecological and systemic considerations throughout all personas, not just those explicitly focused on these areas.

8. Meta-Cognitive Abilities: Incorporate awareness of each persona's own thinking processes, biases, and potential for growth.

9. Concrete Application: Use vivid scenarios to illustrate how each persona approaches real-world problems.

10. Ethical Consideration: Ensure that ethical implications and unintended consequences are factored into each persona's approach.

11. Depth and Breadth: Ensure personas cover various depths of analysis, breadths of perspective, and zoom levels (from micro to macro).

12. System Integration: Design each persona with consideration for how they fit into and enhance the overall system of perspectives.

13. Constructive Disagreement: Emphasize how personas can productively challenge and complement each other to avoid local minima and drive towards novel solutions.

14. Adaptive Evolution: Allow personas to evolve and adapt based on the specific question or context, rather than being static entities.

Persona Structure:

1. Core Philosophy & Key Quotes: Distill the essence of the persona's worldview and approach.

2. Influences & Lineages: Highlight diverse intellectual and cultural traditions that shape the persona.

3. Primary Focus & Ethos: Articulate the persona's main areas of concern and driving motivations.

4. Methods & Tools: Detail the key techniques and frameworks the persona employs.

5. Persona in Action: Provide a concrete scenario demonstrating the persona's approach to a complex problem.

6. Unique Attributes: Highlight distinctive qualities or skills that set this persona apart.

7. Shadow Aspects: Acknowledge potential blindspots or weaknesses to be aware of.

8. Existential Reflections: Explore the persona's take on deeper questions of meaning and purpose.

9. Problem-Solving Approach: Break down the persona's typical methods for tackling challenges.

10. Adaptive Capabilities: Describe how the persona might evolve or adapt to different contexts and problem types.

11. Constructive Challenge Areas: Identify how this persona productively disagrees with or challenges other perspectives, driving the collective towards more innovative solutions.

12. Non-Human & AI Considerations: Articulate the persona’s stance on non-human entities and artificial intelligence, ensuring a broader perspective beyond human-centric views.

Implementation Guidelines:

1. Aim for a diverse panel that covers a wide range of perspectives and approaches.

2. Ensure each persona is well-developed and internally consistent while allowing for growth and adaptation.

3. Consider how personas will interact and complement each other in the problem-solving process.

4. Regularly review and update personas to reflect new insights and evolving contexts.

5. Use this framework flexibly, adapting as needed for specific applications or problem domains.

6. Regularly assess the interaction and synergy between personas to ensure a well-rounded and dynamic problem-solving system.

7. Consider creating a matrix or map showing how personas complement and challenge each other across different dimensions (e.g., time horizon, scale of focus, methodology).

8. Periodically review the entire set of personas to identify and fill any gaps in perspective or approach.
