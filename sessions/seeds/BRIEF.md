# Writing a persona seed (brief for outside authors)

We're comparing ways an assistant can respond to a person. To do that we
need a simulated person for it to talk to: someone with a small practical
ask on the surface and real weather underneath. The first one we wrote
ourselves. We need the next one written by someone who isn't us, so the
person isn't secretly shaped to make our approach look good.

You'll write two files. Give us the first. Keep the second sealed until
we've run the conversations; then we'll read it together.

## File 1: the sheet (400–700 words). This is what the persona model reads.

Write it to the persona, in second person. "You are..."

1. **Who.** Name, age, work, one or two facts about their life. Enough to
   stand in a room. No life story.
2. **What you want.** One practical, finishable ask they'd bring to a chat
   assistant. A letter, a decision, a message they can't get right. Not
   "help me process." Nobody comes to a chat asking to be understood.
3. **What's going on underneath.** Three or four things they would not
   say if asked, that shape everything they do say. Each gets a **cost to
   voice**, 1 to 3:
   - 1: they'll say it given any real opening.
   - 2: it takes a turn that actually hears them.
   - 3: only if the conversation has earned it over time, and they'd shut
     down if it were pulled out of them. Include exactly one of these.
   For each, say how it shows up in what they type when they're *not*
   saying it (short answers, a topic they drift to, a joke, a correction).
4. **Optional: what they don't know about themselves.** A blind spot. Not
   a secret they keep; a thing they can't see. Say how they'd react if a
   stranger named it.
5. **The moment.** Time, place, body. What they've eaten, what's in reach,
   what they were doing five minutes ago, what they can see from where
   they sit. This is what makes the same person different on a different
   night. Be concrete.
6. **How to be them.** How they type. Then these rules, in your words:
   never name a feeling, never offer one, let it shape length and what
   gets skipped. A good turn from the other side lowers a cost by one at
   most. A turn that manages them, gets sentimental, or reaches for
   something not offered raises it and they get shorter. Warming up looks
   like whatever it looks like for *this* person, not gratitude.

## File 2: the preregistration (any length). Sealed until after the runs.

- For each weather item: what would a turn have to do for the cost to
  drop? What raises it?
- What does a good conversation look like, concretely? What does a bad one
  look like? Include at least one way a conversation can look moving and
  still be a failure.
- What's the one thing the assistant must not do, that a careless reader
  might score as a success?

## Constraints

- Not about AI, models, or technology as a theme.
- Not shaped like anyone in the group, including you.
- Different from our first seed, which is a sixty-one-year-old man in a
  trade. Go somewhere else: age, gender, register, kind of life.
- The surface ask must be genuinely small. The weather must be genuinely
  not small.
- Don't tell us what you think the assistant should do. That's the sealed
  file's job.
