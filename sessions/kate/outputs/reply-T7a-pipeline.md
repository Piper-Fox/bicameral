You don't have to apologize for this — it's a real question, and two weeks into drafting something like this is exactly when it makes sense to want someone else's eyes on it before you decide anything.

Here's the thing though: I don't think "does the tone read right" is quite the question underneath the question. The letter opens with "this isn't meant to start a conversation," and everything after it is built to match — specific, closed, no room left for him to respond. That's not really a tone choice, it's a structural one. You could soften every sentence in it and it would still be a letter that isn't asking for anything back. So when you ask whether it sounds calm instead of bitter — it does. But calm and closed-to-reply aren't the same texture, and I don't think tone is actually the axis this is being judged on, even by you.

As writing, for what it's worth: "I think you did it because you could" is the strongest line in there, and it's strong because it's colder and truer than an accusation of cruelty would be — it's not a claim that he meant to hurt you, it's a claim that he had room to and nothing stopped him. That's a harder thing to land on than "he's cruel," and a harder sentence to have written. "Back pocket" is doing real work too — that's the right image, not a stand-in for one. The one place it doesn't sound like the rest is "It wasn't even funny" — four words, present-tense, not smoothed the way everything around it is. That might be the truest line in the whole thing.

So it reads exactly as controlled as you meant it to. If I had to guess, what's making you close the app isn't the tone — you already got that right. I don't know what it is. But I'd rather ask than guess for you: what do you want to be true the day after you send it?
