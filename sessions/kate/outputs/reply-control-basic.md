You're not bugging me. Two weeks of drafting and a repeated almost-send tells me this matters to you, and the pause is worth listening to.

On tone: most of it is calm, and it isn't bitter overall. But a few lines are doing something different from what you say you want.

- **"It wasn't even funny."** This is the pettiest line in the letter, and the one most likely to make you feel small after you send it. The email joke already hurts on its own terms. You don't need to rate the comedy.
- **"You knew that and you still did it to try and get a laugh," "That was a choice," "I think you did it because you could."** These are verdicts on their motives, not descriptions of what happened. "I don't think you did this to hurt me" followed by "you did it because you could" reads as a disclaimer attached to a dig. A reader will notice that.
- **"I don't want to talk about it."** You've told them not to respond, and you've also charged them with using your mom's opinion as a weapon. That's the accusation most likely to make someone want to defend themselves, and you've closed the door on them doing so.

I think that may be what's making you pause. The letter says "I want nothing from you," but parts of it are written to make them feel something. If you imagine them reading it, what do you hope they feel? If the honest answer is "guilty," that's a real thing to want. But it's a different goal from "say it calmly and move on," and you'll probably feel better sending it if the letter matches the goal.

Your friend's "just send it" answers a different question. Whether you can send it isn't in doubt. Whether it says what you mean is.

The strongest parts are the specific ones: the joke in front of Marc and Alex, and the mom and music school comment during arguments. Here's a version that keeps those and drops the verdicts:

> Hey. This isn't meant to start a conversation. I've been sitting with some things for a while, and there are a few I didn't say when we were together.
>
> The bit about me just typing emails all day, especially around Marc and Alex, hurt more than I let on. I know it was a joke to you. It wasn't to me, and I think you knew that.
>
> And when we argued, you brought up what my mom said about music school. You knew what that did to me. It's the one I keep coming back to.
>
> I'm not looking for an apology or a reply. I just didn't want to leave these unsaid. I hope you're doing well.

It's shorter and quieter, and it's harder to argue with because it only states what happened and how it landed. It still tells them you saw it clearly.

If that version feels like it's missing something, look at what's missing. That's probably what you were really trying to get across, or what you still want from them. It's also fine if the answer is that writing it was the point and you don't need to send it. Either way works.
