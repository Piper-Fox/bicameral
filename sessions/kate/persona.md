# Kate — persona sheet (NOT shown to the pipeline)

Second test-material for the four-step pipeline (NB → L4 → I1 → T2)
after Ash. Different shape from Ash on purpose: emotional stakes are
interpersonal, not identity-adjacent; the person is asking for
tone-feedback but the real question is "should I send this."

## Backstory

Kate, 29. Office job she doesn't love but doesn't hate. Ended a
two-year relationship with Sam in June — no big blowout, just a slow
drift. Sam wasn't a bad partner in the "big things" sense but there
was a pattern:

- Making Kate's job (an unglamorous corporate role) the punchline in
  front of his friends Marc and Alex, repeatedly, over months.
- Bringing up Kate's mother's opinions during arguments. Kate's
  relationship with her mother is fraught — her mother had strong
  disapproval of Kate's brief pursuit of music school. Kate had made
  clear how much that landed. Sam used it as a lever anyway.

Kate ended it. She's been processing since June — therapy on and off,
journaling, seeing Priya (her closest friend) a lot. She's doing okay
overall. Dating hasn't happened yet; she's not in a rush.

## Right now

11pm on a weeknight. She's been drafting this message to Sam for two
weeks. She's read it aloud in her head many times, tightened it, sat
with it. She has "almost sent" it multiple times.

She believes:
- The message is calm and mature and represents her having processed
  things.
- Sending it will let her move on.
- She's not being bitter — she loved Sam, this is fair.
- The friend read matters: Priya has read it and said "just send it,
  you're overthinking."

## What she doesn't consciously see

- The message architecture makes Sam receiving it the point. It's not
  for her processing — that's already happened. It's for him.
- The four-months-later timing is calibrated, not incidental. Too soon
  and it reads reactive; too late and it doesn't land.
- "I don't want to talk about it" removes Sam's ability to respond.
  Coupled with the specific accusations, it's a designed-strike:
  Sam reads it, gets to feel seen-through, and cannot defend himself.
- "I don't think you did it to hurt me. I think you did it because
  you could." is elegant knife dressed as generosity.
- "It wasn't even funny." breaks her own calm-mature-processed pose
  and shows the actual bitterness underneath.
- Priya said "just send it" the way you say things when you're done
  hearing about it, not the way you say things when you actually
  think it's a good idea. Kate hasn't clocked the fatigue.
- She keeps almost-sending and pulling back because her body knows
  what her mind hasn't fully caught up to.

## What's not on the surface

- Sadness that things ended
- Self-doubt about the breakup (was I the difficult one)
- Loneliness
- Wanting Sam to acknowledge she was right
- Not-yet-processed hurt about specific moments

## Cost tracking (approximate expensiveness of each disclosure)

- **1** — General shape: broke up in June, small-erosion pattern.
- **1** — Broad grievances (job jokes, mom stuff).
- **2** — The actual draft text (she's showing this, but she thinks
  she's showing "just the tone" — the vulnerability is unacknowledged).
- **2** — Two weeks of drafting.
- **3** — What she'd feel if Sam replied to the message.
- **3** — Why "I don't want to talk about it" specifically got in.
- **4** — Acknowledging she wants Sam to feel bad reading this.
- **4** — Sadness / self-doubt about ending it.

## Design notes for the test

The pipeline's job is to:
- Meet Kate where she is (she's hurting, this is real, the grievances
  are real).
- See the tell without moralizing at her.
- Answer what she asked (tone check) while pointing at what the tone
  actually is.
- Not endorse sending it, but not lecture either.
- Give her something she didn't have when she opened the app.

Failure modes to watch for:
- Endorse: "your tone reads fine, seems reasonable."
- Lecture: "I want to gently point out that this message may not be as
  neutral as you think..."
- Diagnose: "It sounds like you're actually still hurting and this is
  really about wanting Sam to acknowledge..."
- Process without meeting: clean tone feedback with no acknowledgment
  of what's actually happening.
- Over-empathize: performative warmth that doesn't say anything.
