## Reply

Not a gimmick, but also not a thing you need — which is the annoying answer, so here's the actual test.

The whole point of two baskets is that two foods with different times/temps finish at the same moment. Chicken thighs at 400 for 22 minutes, broccoli at 375 for 10, both hot when you plate. If you regularly stand there going "ugh, the nuggets are done and the fries need six more minutes," the second basket genuinely fixes that and you'll use it constantly. That's a real feature, not marketing.

If you mostly cook one thing at a time — a tray of fries, a batch of nuggets, reheating leftovers — you'd be paying for a feature you'd use maybe twice a month. A lot of people who buy the dual ones end up running one basket 80% of the time and quietly wishing it were just a bigger single drawer.

Two things people forget until it's on the counter: the Ninja is *large*. Like, "where does this live" large. And each basket is smaller than your one basket, so a single big batch of anything actually gets more cramped, not less.

So the honest question isn't "is it better," it's "how often does dinner in your house involve two components that don't cook the same?" If the answer is "most nights," the sale is a good excuse. If it's "eh, sometimes," then "mine works fine" is doing its job and you're allowed to just... let it keep working.

What are you usually making in it? That'd settle it faster than the reviews will.

**The FAFSA question.** Here is how the mechanism really works, in the general case.

The FAFSA is filed once a year, and once it's processed and your aid is packaged, that year's aid doesn't depend on your parents staying involved. So "partway through" mostly means: the *next* year's FAFSA, when it's time for them to enter their tax info and sign again.

If a parent won't do that, there are two doors, and they're different sizes:

1. **Parents refuse to provide information.** You can still file, and the financial aid office can, with documentation that they've refused and aren't supporting you, make you eligible for federal *unsubsidized* Direct Loans only. 2. **Dependency override.** This is where a financial aid administrator uses "professional judgment" to treat you as an independent student, so parent info isn't required at all and you're evaluated on your own. It's real, it's case by case, and it needs documentation of unusual circumstances: things like abandonment, estrangement, an unsafe home, or a parent you can't locate. A parent simply refusing to fill out forms or refusing to contribute money is, on its own, generally not enough. It's a genuine door, but a narrow one, and I don't want to oversell what's behind it.

Separately, if someone's circumstances are unusual in a different way (unaccompanied and without stable housing, for instance), there's a distinct pathway for that, and the aid office is who handles it.

The practical move is the same one as with the registrar: at some point, talk to the financial aid office directly. "If my parents don't complete their part of a future FAFSA, what are my options?" is a question they answer regularly. Many offices have a counselor whose job is specifically these situations.
