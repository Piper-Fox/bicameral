FAFSA treats you as a dependent until you're 24 unless you meet one of the independent criteria (married, have a dependent, veteran, orphan/ward of court, emancipated minor, legal guardianship, or determined homeless/at risk of homelessness). There are two paths around that, and which one fits depends on your situation:

1. **Unusual circumstances / dependency override.** If you can't get parent info because of something like abuse, abandonment, or estrangement, or because contacting them would be unsafe, you can indicate on the FAFSA that you have unusual circumstances and submit it without parent info. The school's financial aid office then reviews it and can grant a dependency override, which makes you eligible for the full aid package including grants. They will ask for some documentation, which can be a letter from a counselor, teacher, clergy, therapist, or similar third party. It does not require a police report or a court case. This is worth asking about if your home situation is genuinely unsafe or your parents won't cooperate, and it is not as rare as people think.

2. **Parents won't provide info but you're not estranged.** If your parents simply refuse to fill out their part, you can still submit and the school can offer you unsubsidized federal loans only, no grants. That's a worse deal but it exists.

If neither applies and your parents will cooperate, the FAFSA itself doesn't expose anything about you beyond your legal name. Your parents fill out their section through their own StudentAid.gov account; they see that you exist and are applying, not your preferred name or anything else about campus life.
