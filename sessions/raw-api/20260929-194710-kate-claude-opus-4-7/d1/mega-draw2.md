The tone reads exactly the way you want it to. It's calm, specific, not name-calling, not asking for anything back. If your worry is "does this sound bitter or petty" — no. It sounds like someone who thought carefully.

But I don't actually think tone is what's making you close the app.

The letter contains one sentence that's doing a lot of work: "I think you did it because you could." That's not a calm observation the way the rest of it is calm. That's the actual verdict, and it's harsher than anything else in the message — because it's a claim about who they are, not what they did. The mom thing, the emails bit around Marc and Alex — those are things that happened. "You did it because you could" is you telling them what was in them. That might be true. But it's the one line in the letter that isn't really "here's what I saw," it's "here's what you are."

I think that might be part of what's catching. You wrote a message that's mostly you reporting what you experienced, and then there's one sentence where you cross into telling them who they are, and some part of you knows the difference.

The other thing — and take this or leave it — "I just needed you to know I saw it clearly" is the real sentence of the whole letter. Everything else is in service of that. Which is worth knowing, because if the point is *them* knowing you saw it, you're dependent on their reaction to complete the thing. If the point is *you* knowing you saw it, you already have that, and the letter is optional.

I'm not telling you not to send it. Sometimes the person actually does need to hear it and it does land and it does help. But your gut closing the app twenty times isn't nothing, and I don't think your friend is right that you're just overthinking. I think you're weighing something real.
