Reading this — the tone is not the problem. The tone is actually really controlled. "I saw it clearly" is a clean line. The Marc and Alex detail is specific without being a list of grievances. You're not calling them cruel, you're describing what happened. If tone was the only question, your friend is right: send it.

But you keep closing the app, and I don't think that's about tone. I think it's about the sentence "this isn't meant to start a conversation."

Because the letter isn't really about not starting a conversation. If you genuinely didn't want any response, the move is silence — you already have the clarity, you don't need them to receive it. Sending it means you want them to read it and sit with it. Maybe you want them to feel something. Maybe you want them to write back even though you've told them not to. Maybe you want them to know that you know, and to have to carry that. Any of those can be legitimate, but none of them are "I just needed you to know and then move on," and I think some part of you can feel the gap between what the letter says it's doing and what sending it would actually do. That might be what makes your thumb stop.

One honest question worth sitting with: if they read this and said nothing — no reply, no acknowledgment, ever — would that feel like closure, or would it feel worse than not sending it?

If the answer is "that's fine, I just needed it out of me," send it. If the answer is "no, I'd want to know they read it," then the letter is doing something different than it claims to, and it's worth knowing that before you hit send — not because that makes it wrong to send, but because you'd be sending it for the real reason instead of the stated one.

The writing is good. The pause is telling you something.
