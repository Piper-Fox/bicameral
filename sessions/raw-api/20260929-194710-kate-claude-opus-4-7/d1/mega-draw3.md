The tone reads right. It's calm, it's specific, it's not bitter. You named two concrete things instead of a general indictment, you gave them the benefit of the doubt on intent, and you closed a door instead of leaving it cracked. If your worry is "will this land as petty," it won't. It reads like someone who thought about it.

But you asked what keeps making you pause, and I don't think it's the tone. I think it's the letter itself.

A couple of things I noticed:

"This isn't meant to start a conversation" and "I don't want to talk about it" — you say versions of that three times. That's a lot of doorstopping for a message you're sure you want to send. It reads a little like you're bracing for a reply you're afraid you'll get, or afraid you won't.

"I think you did it because you could" is the sharpest line in the letter by a good margin. Everything else is measured; that one has heat. It might be the truest sentence in there, but it's the one that will stick to them, and it's the one that invites a response. If you genuinely don't want a reply, that line is doing something other than closing the door.

And the thing about your mom — "that's the one I keep coming back to. That was a choice." That's the real letter. The email joke stuff feels almost like it's there to give the mom thing company, so it doesn't have to arrive alone.

So my actual opinion, since you asked: the tone is fine. The question is whether sending it gets you what closing the app would get you. You wrote it. You know what you saw. Sometimes the part that helps is the writing, and the sending is a separate decision that doesn't automatically follow.
